In-Context Imitation Learning:
Instant Policy ++

Extending In-Context Imitation Learning with hyperbolic embeddings and product-manifold attention to capture hierarchical action semantics in robotic tasks.

Teoh Kai Shun  ·  Supervised by Dr Edward Johns  ·  Department of Computing

GitHub Demo Data Slides Instant Policy Paper

Overview

Abstract

Recent advancements in non-Euclidean representation learning have motivated applications to robotics. This work proposes a potential improvement for Instant Policy — a recent approach to In-Context Imitation Learning (ICIL) that enables rapid learning of robot tasks from just 1–2 demonstrations — by incorporating hyperbolic geometry.

We introduce a dual-channel architecture: a Euclidean channel that captures spatial features, and a Hyperbolic channel that encodes the hierarchical semantic structure of action trajectories via Sarkar's constructor embedded in the Poincaré ball. The two channels are fused through a Product Manifold Attention (PMA) layer that preserves the geometric properties of each space while enabling aligned, context-conditioned action prediction.

Experiments on 2D manipulation tasks benchmark the proposed model against the Instant Policy baseline, and a linear probe study analyses whether hyperbolic embeddings encode sequential action semantics.

Design

Architecture

The model extends Instant Policy with a parallel hyperbolic processing stream. The three key innovations are the dual-channel design, geometry-preserving fusion, and training-free hyperbolic embeddings via Sarkar's constructor.

🌐

Euclidean Channel (adapted from IP)

Processes 2D point clouds via simplified PointNet++, constructing local graphs with Hetero-GAT aggregation. Captures spatial geometry at each timestep — temporally-agnostic embeddings grounding each action to its physical state.

⬡

Hyperbolic Channel (novel)

Clusters action trajectories into hierarchical trees by orientation/state transitions, then embeds them in the Poincaré ball via Sarkar's constructor. A Hyperbolic GAT with a depth-dependent linear gate propagates temporal awareness.

✦

Product Manifold Attention

Lifts hyperbolic embeddings to tangent space and fuses them with Euclidean embeddings via a shared latent interface. Learnt mixture weights (λE, λH) adaptively blend Euclidean spatial cues with hyperbolic semantic cues.

🌲

Sarkar's Constructor

Training-free, combinatorial embedding of any finite tree into the Poincaré ball with arbitrarily low distortion (ε-bounded). Provides control over embedding depth, avoiding vanishing gradients near the ball boundary.

〜

Diffusion Denoising

Like Instant Policy, the model learns per-node denoising directions under a DDIM scheduler. At deployment, noisy actions are iteratively refined over 1000 steps conditioned on real human demonstrations.

⌥

Pseudo Demonstrations

Training uses procedurally generated pseudo-demonstrations (random and biased types) — an infinite source of semantically consistent data. No human demonstrations are required at training time.

Linear Probe Study

Do hyperbolic embeddings encode next-action semantics?

A frozen linear probe on hyperbolic embeddings achieved 18.5% test accuracy — below both token-only baselines (Bigram: 36.6%, Last-token: 26.9%). A 2-layer MLP reached 34.0%, suggesting some non-linear structure exists but is insufficient. The core assumption — that hyperbolic embeddings linearly capture fine-grained action semantics — was not validated in this iteration.

Analysis

Limitations & Findings

Honest post-hoc analysis of failure modes — including feedback from the presentation that isn't in the report.

Demo Distribution Gap

Pseudo-demonstrations are procedurally generated, making movements more sudden and "perfect" than real human data. Despite accounting for variation in generation, the sampling step trades off information completeness for computational tractability — causing the distribution seen at training to differ from the smoother, noisier human demos at evaluation. This train/eval mismatch hurt generalisation.

Embeddings Lack Local Dynamics

Instant Policy's end-to-end denoising objective directly models fine-grained local action dynamics. The frozen hyperbolic embeddings, built from coarse angular-granularity clusters, discard the local step-by-step signal that the diffusion head needs. The semantic signature is global — it describes trajectory type, not next-step action.

Geometry Encoder

The simplified PointNet++ replacement produced convergent occupancy loss (~0.03) but its Euclidean embeddings may lack sufficient expressiveness compared to the full PointNet++ used in the original Instant Policy — particularly for 2D scene geometry, where point-cloud direction variance is limited to a single plane.

Demonstration Downsampling

Demonstrations were evenly downsampled to a fixed max length for computational efficiency. Unlike Instant Policy's frame-selection strategy that preserves semantically significant frames, even downsampling on longer tasks (Tasks C & D, 40–80 steps) discards the fine-grained transitions that carry the most task-relevant information.

Roadmap

Future Directions

The current prototype reveals what works and what doesn't. These are the natural next steps.

  • 01 Strengthen local dynamics in embeddings. Add lightweight sequence-predictive or contrastive auxiliary objectives during hyperbolic embedding construction — so that embeddings retain fine-grained temporal signal, not just global trajectory type. This directly addresses the linear probe failure.
  • 02 Human-demo-aware pseudo-demonstration sampling. Reformulate pseudo-demo sampling to better approximate the motion characteristics of human demonstrations — smoother trajectories with realistic acceleration profiles — so the train/eval distribution gap is minimised without sacrificing computational tractability.
  • 03 Tree-structured multi-resolution generation. Represent demonstrations as tree-structured semantic signatures and employ a coarse-to-fine graph-generation model. For any given task, the underlying sparse distribution generates semantic waypoints of variable length; tree-based representations with hierarchical generation could exploit this structure for more robust ICIL.
  • 04 Semantically-aware frame selection. Adopt Instant Policy's strategy of selecting "keyframe" demonstration frames rather than even downsampling. This is especially important for longer tasks (C, D) where semantic transitions are concentrated in short sub-sequences that even downsampling would discard.
  • 05 Spherical channel for cyclical tasks. The product manifold framework is designed to be extensible. Tasks like balancing exhibit near-cyclical patterns suited to spherical geometry — adding an S² component to the product manifold is a natural direction once the dual-channel baseline is stable.
